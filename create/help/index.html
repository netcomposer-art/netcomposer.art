How to write a composition

The Composition field is the box on Create. What you type there is what the page reads. Copy any example below straight into that box.

A beat is one count. A bar is a group of beats. Unless you write another time, a bar has four beats. That time is called 4/4.

Above the Composition field, the tempo and the time signature are already filled in: 96 and 4/4. They are there even when the field is empty. Change either box, or write the same thing in the field. The field is what is kept. A number you have not written looks a little quieter.

Play is the button that sounds what you wrote. With no @name, that is the built-in synth, including a chord such as Cm11. @piano, @guitar, and @cello play the sampler. Gather finds a recording for each chord. Perform puts those recordings into the piece. Keep writes one fixed file of what you heard. Live holds each filter, and the other effects, at the number you wrote.

What to type first

Start with one chord. A capital letter is the root. m after it means minor. Cm is C minor.

Cm

Play sketches one bar of C minor, about 2.5 seconds, at 96 BPM. Gather uses the whole recording. The line under the field asks you to write a length. The tempo above the field stays 96, because you did not write one.

A sketch is the stand-in Play uses when you did not write a length. One bar, on the built-in synth. BPM means beats per minute, which is the speed. 96 BPM is the speed used when you have not written one. It is already showing above the field.

90 BPM Cm(2)

Two beats of C minor at 90 BPM, about 1.3 seconds. That is half a bar, shorter than the one-bar sketch above. The length and the speed are both written, so the line under the field adds nothing.

Chords

A chord is a named set of notes. The root is the letter it is built on. You can write the root in lowercase when a quality follows it. A quality is the part after the letter, such as m or maj7.

fm9

F minor 9, the same chord as Fm9. Play sketches one bar at 96 BPM. Gather uses the whole recording.

C

C major, one sketched bar at 96 BPM. A letter with nothing after it is major.

A single lowercase letter on its own is a word, not a chord. c is not C major. Write C.

Write the root as C, Db, D, Eb, E, F, F#, G, Ab, A, Bb, or B. C# is read as Db. A# is read as Bb. F# stays F#.

C#m

C sharp minor, read as Dbm. Play sketches one bar of that chord at 96 BPM.

Cm/G

C minor with G in the bass. The note after the slash is the bottom note. Play sketches one bar.

A few short forms mean the same chord. Cmin and C- are Cm. CM7 is Cmaj7. Csus is Csus4. C6/9 is the 6/9 chord. C2 is Csus2. C+ is Caug.

CM7

C major 7, read from the short form CM7. Play sketches one bar at 96 BPM.

These are the qualities you can write after a root. The examples use C. Any root from the list above works the same way.

  • C major
  • Cm minor
  • C6 major 6
  • C6-9 or C6/9 six-nine
  • C7 dominant 7
  • C7#9 dominant 7, sharp 9
  • C7#11 dominant 7, sharp 11. An arpeggio cannot play this one. On an arp line, write C7 in its place.
  • C7alt altered dominant
  • C7b5 dominant 7, flat 5
  • C7b9 dominant 7, flat 9
  • C7b13 dominant 7, flat 13. An arpeggio cannot play this one either. Write C7 on the arp line.
  • C7sus4 or C7sus suspended 7
  • C9 dominant 9
  • C9sus4 or C9sus suspended 9
  • Cadd9 or Cadd2 add 9
  • Caug or C+ augmented
  • Cdim diminished
  • Cdim7 diminished 7
  • Cm6 minor 6
  • Cm6-9 minor 6/9
  • Cm7 minor 7
  • Cm7b5 half-diminished
  • Cm9 minor 9
  • Cm11 minor 11
  • Cm13 minor 13
  • Cm-add9 minor add 9
  • Cm-maj7 minor-major 7
  • Cmaj7 major 7
  • Cmaj7#5 major 7, sharp 5
  • Cmaj7#11 major 7, sharp 11
  • Cmaj9 major 9
  • Cmaj13 major 13
  • Csus2 or C2 suspended 2
  • Csus4 or Csus suspended 4

A near miss is named in the line under the field. Cmaj13b is not a chord. The line asks if you meant Cmaj13.

Lengths

A length belongs to the chord it is glued to. The parentheses sit on that chord. (2) means 2 beats. (4) means 4 beats, which is one bar, the same length as the sketch. (8) means 8 beats, which is two bars. (4|) means 4 bars. The bar mark is the vertical line inside the parentheses.

90 BPM Cm(2)

Two beats of C minor at 90 BPM, about 1.3 seconds. Gather uses two beats of the recording. It is shorter than the one-bar sketch.

90 BPM Cm(4|)

Four bars of C minor at 90 BPM. That is 16 beats, about 10.7 seconds.

Cm

No length is written. Play sketches one bar, about 2.5 seconds, at 96 BPM. Gather uses the whole recording.

Cm(?)

The same split, written on purpose. Play sketches one bar. Gather plays the whole recording. The question mark inside the parentheses is that mark.

A rest is silence. An underscore stands for the rest. It takes a length, the same way a chord does.

90 BPM [Cm(8) _(2) Fm9(2)]

C minor for 8 beats, then 2 beats of silence, then F minor 9 for 2 beats. 8 seconds at 90 BPM.

Tempo

Tempo is the speed, in beats per minute. Write it at the start. 90 BPM, tempo 90, and q90 are the same speed. The q stands for the quarter note, which is one beat.

90 BPM Cm(2)

Two beats of C minor at 90 per minute, about 1.3 seconds.

tempo 90 Cm(2)

Two beats of C minor at the same speed, about 1.3 seconds.

q90 Cm(2)

The same two beats as 90 BPM, written with q90.

If you write no tempo, the box above the field stays at 96, in a quieter grey. Play uses that 96. Type another number there, or write 90 BPM at the start. Both write the same statement. Put 96 back and that statement goes away. A second tempo later in the box is not read. Keep the one at the start and delete the other.

Time signature

The time signature is how many beats are in a bar. It sits next to the tempo, and it starts at 4/4, even on an empty field. A beat on this page is a quarter note. 3/4 is three beats. 6/8 counts as 3 beats in a bar. 2/2 is four beats.

Type it in that box, or write it at the start, after the tempo if you wrote one. A number you have not written looks quieter. Put 4/4 back and a written time goes away. One sketched bar, and (1|), both last that many beats. In 6/8 that is 3 beats, so (3) is one bar and (6) is two. The beats before a bar line must add up to one bar.

90 BPM 3/4 [Cm(3) | Fm(3)]

Two bars of 3/4 at 90 BPM. C minor for one bar, then F minor for one bar. 6 beats, 4 seconds.

6/8 Cm(1|)

One bar of 6/8. That is 3 beats, about 1.9 seconds at 96 BPM.

6/8 [Cm(2) G(1) | Am(3)]

Cm takes 2 beats and G takes 1, which is one bar of 6/8. Am(3) is the next bar. 6 beats, about 3.8 seconds at 96 BPM.

3/4 Cm

No length is written. Play sketches one bar of 3/4, which is 3 beats, about 1.9 seconds at 96 BPM. Gather uses the whole recording.

Groups and repeats

Square brackets wrap a group. x4 after the group plays it four times. loop(4) on the next line does the same thing. Each repeat uses the same recordings.

[Cm Fm9]x4

The two chords play four times at 96 BPM. Neither has a length, so Play sketches each as one bar and Gather uses each whole recording. 32 beats, 20 seconds.

90 BPM [Cm(2) Fm9(2|)]x4

C minor for 2 beats, then F minor 9 for 2 bars, and that pair four times. 40 beats, about 26.7 seconds. Play shows the pair once, with ×4, not eight separate chords.

90 BPM
[Cm(2) Fm9(2|)]
loop(4)

The same 40 beats, about 26.7 seconds, as the line above. loop(4) is the long way to write x4.

A repeat needs a whole number, and it needs a group to wrap. Write x4 or loop(4) just after the closing bracket. One pair of brackets is read. A group inside a group is not.

A bar line, the | character, splits a group into bars. The beats before each bar line must add up to one bar. In 4/4 that is 4 beats. Every chord before the line needs its own length.

90 BPM [Cm(2) Fm(2) | Gm(4)]

C minor and F minor share the first bar, 2 beats each. G minor takes the next bar. About 5.3 seconds at 90 BPM.

Notes

A notes line is a melody, written as pitches. A pitch is a letter plus an octave number, such as C5. C5 on its own is not a chord. Start the line with n or notes, and give it at least one pitch. The line runs to the end of that line.

Glue a length to a pitch the same way as a chord. (2) is 2 beats. (2|) is 2 bars. A pitch with no length just follows.

n C5(2) Eb5 G5 Bb4

Play lists the line and stays quiet. Gather renders C5, E flat 5, G5, and B flat 4 on the built-in synth. C5 lasts 2 beats. Perform plays that melody.

notes C4 Eb4 G4(2)

The same kind of line, with the word notes. G4 lasts 2 beats. Play stays quiet. Gather and Perform use the synth.

n C5(2|) Eb5(1)

C5 lasts 2 bars. E flat 5 lasts 1 beat. Again, Play only lists it.

notes create melody

Nothing plays. There is no pitch, so the line stays words for the AI. A notes line needs a pitch such as C5.

FM

FM is frequency modulation. Here it is a colour on the synth, not a chord. fm 3 is a depth from 0 to 10. A higher number is read as 10. fm3 is the same depth. fm9 on its own is the chord F minor 9, because those letters are a chord.

Write the depth on a notes line, or on an arp line. An arp is an arpeggio: the notes of a chord played one at a time.

n C5(2) Eb5 G5 fm 3

The same pitches as a plain notes line, with FM depth 3. Play lists it. Gather renders it on the synth with that depth. Perform plays it.

fm9

This one is the chord. F minor 9, one sketched bar at 96 BPM, not an FM depth.

arp Cm9 fm 3

C minor 9 as an upward arpeggio, with FM depth 3. Play lists it. Gather renders it on the synth.

Arpeggio

Start the line with arp. The chord is optional. With no chord, the arpeggio follows the chords you wrote above it. A length on this line does not change those chords.

What you do not write is filled in: up, sixteenths, one octave, notes close together, no inversion. An inversion is the chord flipped so another note sits on the bottom. An octave is the same note eight letters higher or lower.

arp Cm9

C minor 9, notes one at a time, lowest to highest, four notes per beat. Play lists it and stays quiet. Gather renders it on the synth.

Order is the sequence of the notes. You can write the name with a slash. up/down is the same as up-down.

arp Cm9 down

The same chord, highest note to lowest.

arp Cm9 up-down

Up, then back down. The top note is not struck twice.

arp Cm9 up-down-repeat

Up, then down, and the turning notes are struck again.

arp Cm9 outside-in

Lowest, then highest, then the next ones in from the edges.

arp Cm9 inside-out

From the middle of the chord out toward the edges.

arp Cm9 pendulum

The up-and-down path, starting in the middle.

arp Cm9 random

A note of the chord chosen at random for each step.

arp Cm9 as-played

The notes in the order the chord is written, not sorted low to high.

arp Cm9 down-up

The reverse of up-down. down/up is the same name.

Rate is how fast the notes go. 4ths or quarter is one note per beat. 8ths or eighth is two. 16ths is four, and it is the usual rate. 32nds is eight. A triplet squeezes three notes into the time of two of that value. 8t is three notes in one beat. 4t, 16t, and 32t are the slower and faster triplets.

arp Cm9 4ths

One note per beat, going up.

arp Cm9 8ths

Two notes per beat, going up.

arp Cm9 32nds

Eight notes per beat, going up.

arp Cm9 8t

Three notes in each beat, going up.

arp Cm9 4t

A quarter-note triplet: three notes in the time of two beats.

arp Cm9 16t

A sixteenth-note triplet, faster than 8t.

arp Cm9 32t

The fastest triplet, three notes in the time of two thirty-second notes.

Octaves stack the chord higher. Write 2oct or 2 octaves. The number is from 1 to 4.

arp Cm9 2oct

The arpeggio climbs across two octaves.

arp Cm9 4oct

The same climb across four octaves, which is as far as this line goes.

Spread is how far apart the notes sit. compact is the close one, and it is the usual spread. Then wide, wider, and max. maximum is the same as max.

arp Cm9 wide

The notes sit further apart than the usual close voicing.

arp Cm9 wider

Further apart again.

arp Cm9 max

The widest spread. maximum is the same word.

An inversion flips the chord. inv1 and inversion 1 are the first flip.

arp Cm9 inv1

C minor 9 turned once, so a different note is on the bottom, then arpeggiated upward.

arp Cm9 (2)

The arpeggio runs for 2 beats. The chord line, if you wrote one, keeps its own lengths.

arp Cm9 (2|)

The arpeggio runs for 2 bars.

90 BPM
[Cm(2) Fm9(8)]x2
arp

Play sketches the two chords, twice, at 90 BPM: C minor for 2 beats, then F minor 9 for 8 beats, about 13.3 seconds, and lists the arp. The arp has no chord of its own, so it follows Cm and then Fm9. Gather fetches those two recordings and renders the arp on the synth.

arp Cm9 pendulum 16ths 2oct wide inv1 (2|)

One line with the options together: pendulum, sixteenths, two octaves, wide, first inversion, for 2 bars. Gather renders that on the synth.

Style

A style line is not on this page yet. When it is, it will read style and then a name, and this section will say what that fills in.

Words for the AI

Words this page does not recognise are left for the AI. They are not played. Quotes force a phrase to stay words, even when it looks like something else. The line under the field tells you so, and shows the quotes to type.

90 BPM Cm(2) let it breathe

You hear two beats of C minor at 90 BPM, about 1.3 seconds. The words are not notes. They are left for the AI.

90 BPM Cm(2) "hold the high note"

The same two beats. The quoted phrase stays words for the AI. It is not read as chords.

A chord inside quotes stays words too, and it does not play. The line under the field tells you to delete the quotes around it. An unclosed quote asks you to add a " at the end.

An instrument

Play sounds the built-in synth. @piano, @guitar, and @cello play the sampler instead, on a notes line, an arp, or a chord line. On a chord line the sampler plays that chord's voicing. Any other name, such as @flute, only labels the line, and Play uses the synth. Gather still finds a recording, unless the line says @synth. A notes line or an arp with no track label is listed, and Play stays quiet.

Cm11

Play sounds C minor 11 on the built-in synth, one sketched bar at 96 BPM, from the catalog voicing. Gather still finds a recording.

Cm11 @piano

Play sounds that same C minor 11 voicing on the piano sampler. The built-in synth stays out. The piano notes need a connection once. Gather still finds a recording.

n C5@piano Eb5

Play lists the pitches and the piano name, and stays quiet. There is no track label, so the sampler does not play this line.

arp Cm9 @flute

The arpeggio is kept, and the name flute only labels it. Play lists it and stays quiet. There is no track label.

Tracks

A track label is t1:, t2:, and so on. Write it at the start of a line, or several on one line. Everything after a label, up to the next label or the end of that line, belongs to that track. The same label again adds to that track. T1 and t1 are the same track. A line with no label is read the way it always was.

Tracks start together. They share the tempo and the time signature. Each one can be a chord progression, a notes line, or an arp, with its own lengths and its own FM depth. Each synth track is its own voice. On Play, a chord track sounds the built-in synth. @piano, @guitar, and @cello play the sampler, and on a chord track the sampler plays that chord's voicing. A plain name, such as @flute, only labels the track, and Play uses the synth. @synth is the same synth, and Gather does not fetch a recording for it.

Play sounds a synth track, and a sampler track, from its notes as soon as you press Play. There is no render wait. A piece that is only the built-in synth plays with no connection. The sampler needs a connection once. Gather still finds a recording for a chord that is not @synth.

t1: Cm(4|) Fm(4|) @synth
t2: notes C5(4|) G5(4|) fm 2

Play sounds both from their notes as soon as you press it, with no render wait. T1 is C minor for 4 bars, then F minor for 4 bars, on the synth. T2 is C5 for 4 bars, then G5 for 4 bars, with FM depth 2 written on the line. They start together. 32 beats, 20 seconds at 96 BPM. Gather does not fetch a recording. This piece plays with no connection.

t1: Cm(4|) t2: Em(4|)

Play sounds both on the built-in synth, from the catalog voicing, one lane each. T1 is C minor and T2 is E minor, both for 4 bars, starting together. 16 beats, 10 seconds at 96 BPM. Gather still fetches those recordings, and they need a connection once.

t1: notes C4(4|) @piano

Play sounds C4 for 4 bars on the piano, from the note, with no render wait. The built-in synth stays out. 16 beats, 10 seconds at 96 BPM. The piano notes need a connection once.

t1: notes C4(4|) @flute

T1 keeps C4 for 4 bars. The name flute only labels it. Play sounds C4 on the built-in synth. 16 beats, 10 seconds at 96 BPM. This piece plays with no connection.

t1: Cm11(4)
t2: notes G3(4) @cello

Play sounds T1, C minor 11 for 4 beats, on the built-in synth, and T2, G3 for 4 beats, on the cello sampler. They start together. 4 beats, 2.5 seconds at 96 BPM. The cello notes need a connection once. Gather still finds a recording for C minor 11.

Composition hacks

A hack is a starting effect for the whole piece. Write it outside the brackets. Live hears it. Play still sketches the chords dry, with no effect.

The filter names are lowpass (lp), highpass (hp), and bandpass (bp). A low-pass lets the low sounds through. A high-pass lets the high sounds through. A band-pass keeps a band in the middle. The number is a frequency in hertz, written Hz.

90 BPM Cm(2) lp500

Play sketches two beats of C minor, dry. Live starts with a low-pass filter at 500 Hz.

90 BPM Cm(2) lowpass 800

The same idea, written as words. It is read as lp800. Live starts the low-pass at 800 Hz.

90 BPM Cm(2) lp500->200

Live parks the low-pass at 500 Hz. The arrow is written as ->, and I-read keeps it. Play still sketches the chord dry.

The other hacks take a number too. eq is a mid boost in decibels. dl or delay is an echo, from 0 to 100. rv or reverb is a plate, which is a kind of room, from 0 to 100. dr or drive is extra push, from 0 to 24. wd or width is the stereo width, from 0 to 200. 100 is the normal width. cp or comp is a compressor, which evens loud and quiet. Write cp0. A minus number is not read yet.

90 BPM Cm(2) hp200 bp1000 eq2 dl20 rv15 dr6 wd100

Play sketches the chord dry. Live starts with a high-pass at 200 Hz, a band-pass around 1000 Hz, a small mid boost, a little delay, a little plate, a little drive, and normal width.

Write each hack once. If you write the same one twice, the later number wins, and the line under the field tells you to delete the earlier one. A hack inside the brackets is not for that chord. Move it outside, at the start.

The I-read line

Under the Composition field, a line begins “I read”. It is the page saying what it understood. The first line is the music, and it names what Play will sound: synth, or @piano, @guitar, or @cello. Then any words left for the AI. Then a note, only when you may want to change something. A note always includes the exact text to type, in a grey box, and says where it goes. A How link on that note opens the part of this page that matches.

A star on a length, written 1 bar*, means you did not write that length. Play sketches one bar. Gather uses the whole recording.

Cm

I-read says: 96 BPM, Cm as 1 bar*, 4 beats, 2.5 seconds, and synth. Then it asks you to write Cm(4) for 4 beats or Cm(2|) for 2 bars. The 96 is already in the tempo box, so it does not ask you to add a tempo. You hear one sketched bar on the synth.

90 BPM [Cm(2) Fm9(2|)]x4

I-read says: 90 BPM, the pair times 4, 40 beats, about 26.7 seconds. There is no note, because the speed and both lengths are written. You hear 2 beats, then 2 bars, four times.

A chord the page does not know is a note too. It suggests the nearest real chord when it can, such as Cmaj13 for Cmaj13b.

Play, Gather, and Perform

Play sounds the chords you wrote on the built-in synth, once each, at the speed you wrote. A group repeat is shown once, with a times mark, and Play still goes around it that many times. Play does not fill the piece out to two minutes. A notes line or an arp with no track label is listed, and Play stays quiet. Play does not sound a hack. @piano, @guitar, and @cello play the sampler, including the voicing of a chord line. Any other @name only labels the line, and Play uses the synth. A synth track or a sampler track plays live from its notes, with no render wait. Gather still finds the recordings. Play does not play those recordings.

Gather finds one recording per chord. A written length uses that much of the recording. No length uses the whole recording. A repeat reuses the same recording. A notes line or an arp with no @instrument is rendered here on the built-in synth, and listed with the recordings.

Perform puts what Gather found into the piece, with the synth and sampler tracks. Keep writes one fixed file of what you heard. Live parks each hack at the number you wrote. A low-pass written lp500->200 stays at 500 Hz.

A piece that is only the built-in synth plays with no connection. The sampler, and the recordings, need a connection once.

90 BPM
[Cm(2) Fm9 Gm7(8)]x4
n C5(2) Eb5 G5 Bb4 fm 3
arp Cm9 pendulum 16ths 2oct wide inv1 (2|)
lp500->200
"keep it close"

Play sketches the three chords four times at 90 BPM, about 37.3 seconds, on the built-in synth: C minor for 2 beats, F minor 9 as one bar, G minor 7 for 8 beats. It also lists the notes, the arp, and the filter, and those stay quiet. Gather fetches the three recordings, renders the melody and the arp on the synth with FM depth 3, and keeps the quoted words as words. Perform plays the recordings and the synth. Live parks the low-pass at 500 Hz.

Also on the line

A # starts a comment. The rest of that line is ignored. It is not heard, and it is not left for the AI.

90 BPM Cm(2) # two beats

Two beats of C minor at 90 BPM. The words after the # are dropped.

A seed is a number that later choices can reuse. sd 4182 and seed 4182 are the same. The first seed is kept. A later different seed is a note, and it tells you to delete the later one.

sd 4182
90 BPM Cm(2)

Two beats of C minor at 90 BPM. The seed is remembered as sd4182. It does not change this sketch.
