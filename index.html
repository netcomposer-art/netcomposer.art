NetComposer

Music performed by the open internet.

“Music’s structure can be described mathematically. There is art in the math, and there is art in the performance. NetComposer is my exploration of that duality.”

— Petrus Semmelhack

A Composition sets the harmony, structure, and tempo shape. A Performer realizes it from openly licensed audio the crawlers find. Every performance leaves a public record of what was used, where it came from, and who made it.

The record lists every input with its checksum, every setting and the seed, so each performance can be rebuilt exactly.

One Composition, three Performances

The C major Prelude, harmony only (after Bach, BWV 846). Composed by Petrus from the When in Rome analysis (CC BY-SA 4.0). One chord per bar, 35 bars, 4/4, 69 BPM, broadening over bars 33–35. No notes, no timbres. Composition text.

Roles

  • Suppliers share audio under open licenses and are credited in every record that uses it.
  • Composers write Compositions: harmony, bars, and tempo shape, with no fixed notes or timbres.
  • Performers realize a Composition from what the crawlers find, extended with hacks.
  • Audience hears the result, released CC BY-SA 4.0 to share and remix.

Status

Today the Performer draws on one dedicated Supplier, musicmatter.art, plus Freesound. The wider Supplier network is the ambition. Inputs must be CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA, or public domain; never NC, ND, Sampling+, or custom licenses. Time-stretch is allowed; pitch-shifting is not. Records are unsigned for now.

First Supplier: musicmatter.art

musicmatter.art by Petrus Semmelhack is the first NetComposer Supplier: 1,356 lossless sounds (FLAC, 24-bit/48 kHz), 702 chord strikes and 654 up/down arpeggios, across 12 roots and 9 qualities (maj, min, 7, maj7, m7, dim, dim7, aug, sus4), in multiple takes. License: CC BY-SA 4.0.

Machine-readable: an index at musicmatter.art/netcomposer.json lists every sound with its FLAC and a per-sound manifest (/sounds/<name>.nc.json: chord, register and bass, format, sha256, license).

How the Performer uses it: it reads the manifests, picks one sound per bar whose chord matches the Composition, prefers the one whose lowest note is the written bass and whose register sits close to the previous bar, then trims, layers or time-stretches it (never pitch-shifts). Performance 1 uses 28 chord strikes; performance 2 uses 26 arpeggios. Both were read from a local copy of the site, and the audio used is sample-identical to the published FLACs. Performance 3 uses Freesound only.
